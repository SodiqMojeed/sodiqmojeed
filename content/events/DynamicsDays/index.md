I attended a two-week CIMPA School on Topological and Extremal Properties of Combinatorial Structures at the University of Abomey-Calavi in July 2026, where I broadened my knowledge of topics in graph theory and combinatorics, including extremal problems, graph algorithms, and spectral graph theory. I also gave a flash talk on my research titled "A Network-Based Intervention for Optimizing Interactions among Early-Career Scientists".

Abstract: Past research has emphasized the importance of team diversity for innovation
and scientific collaboration. Yet, little is known about how to eƯiciently catalyze the
assembling of novel interdisciplinary groups at summer schools and conferences with
multiple breakout sessions. This research introduces a network-based group formation
algorithm that maximizes group diversity and minimizes the number of repeated
meetings among attendees. The algorithm assigns participants to culturally and
intellectually diverse groups, with fewer repeated meetings between pairs across
multiple sessions. Simulation results show that the algorithm outperforms random group
assignments in reducing the frequency of repeated meetings among conference
participants, minimizing group prior knowledge, and maximizing group diversity with
respect to participants’ gender, discipline, and country of residence. This network-based
intervention provides a unique mechanism to optimize group assignments at
conferences and summer schools, fostering novel, interdisciplinary collaborations. We
validate the algorithm using interaction data from >120 students at two Santa Fe Institute
Complexity Global Schools and recommend its use at conferences and summer schools
to catalyze scientific collaboration and innovation.
